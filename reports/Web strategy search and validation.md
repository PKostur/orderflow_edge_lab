# Nine published strategies tested, none cleared the gates

**No strategy passed all eight gates, so the search produced no promising prospect.** Nine web-published strategies were coded exactly as published and run once each (7 in round 1, 2 in round 2), and 0 of 9 passed the deflated-Sharpe gate (G5); the 31 unused trials stay unused. The search is closed with a FAIL verdict, as the pre-registered protocol requires. The nine split into three failure types. The ETF allocation models (VAA, PAA, GEM) and the oil-timing rule earned mostly exposure: random books with the same structure earned 50 to 100% of their return. The SPY calendar rules decayed after their publication samples, and turn-of-month was the only candidate that failed G5 alone. Two prominent ideas failed in instructive ways: CTREND, the 2025 crypto trend factor, lost money in its 2020-10 to 2022-05 development window (Sharpe -2.4, t -2.6) and made money after it (Sharpe 0.95), while the FOMC-cycle rule passed its random-timing null but failed validation, G5 and independence from SPY. The binding constraint is statistical: the G5 hurdle grew from about 1.3 to about 1.85 annualized Sharpe as trials rose from 7 to 9 and their dispersion doubled, while published ideas of this kind cap near net Sharpe 1.0. The honest options are descriptive forward watches pre-registered from today, or a new, separately pre-registered search with fewer and higher-prior candidates. Neither supports live trading.

## The protocol fixed every gate before any candidate was seen

The rules were registered on 2026-10-11, before any candidate was collected, because the user asked to keep searching until something passed, and searching until something passes is a multiple-testing machine (`config/strategy_search_v1.json`). A candidate was eligible only if its source gave explicit, codable entry, exit, sizing, universe and rebalance rules and it could be run on free daily data. Each eligible candidate was coded once with the source's own parameters; the only allowed fix was a bug exposed by the look-ahead or accounting checks, and it had to be logged. The budget was 40 trials, in rounds of up to 12 candidates, with at most 4 rounds, stopping at the first candidate to pass everything. Every round was registered before its candidates were backtested and run once, with a single look. Families that had already failed in the repo (price-only zoos, funding carry, new trend variants on the repo's DON8/EMA8/VOL8 rules or its 10 coins) were excluded, and candidates adding assets outside the crypto factor or a new information type were preferred.

Eight gates then applied to each candidate. G1 required signals at date t to be unchanged when all later data is removed, checked on 50 random dates. G2 required a development net Sharpe of at least 0.5 and a Newey-West t (5 lags) of at least 2.0. G3 required an untouched validation window (2020-01-01 to 2026-09-30) with Sharpe at least 0.4, the same sign, and at least 40% of the development Sharpe. G4 required the development mean to beat 999 action-matched random books at an empirical p of 0.05 or less: allocation models were compared with random asset assignments carrying the same weight vectors, timing rules with random placements of the same number of invested days and spell lengths. G5 required a deflated-Sharpe probability of at least 0.95, with the trial count equal to every candidate tested so far, cumulative across rounds. G6 required every published parameter, moved by about 20% one at a time, to keep at least half the base Sharpe, with both halves of the development sample positive. G7 required a cost wall (the round-trip cost at which the mean net return reaches zero) of at least 3 times the assumed cost, which was 10 bps for ETFs and 20 bps for crypto plus the venue's funding. G8 required daily correlation of at most 0.5 with the repo's crypto trend core and, for non-crypto candidates, at most 0.7 with SPY. The outcome clause says a pass would only be a frozen forward watch, not a promotion, and that exhausting the budget reports FAIL "with the full trial ledger" and is not extended or rerun with looser gates.

Data substitutions were logged rather than hidden. Round 1 used Yahoo adjusted closes and FRED series, with uninvested cash earning zero (conservative). The oil rule used FRED WTI and T-bill series because the QuantConnect page's OPEC and bill datasets are discontinued. The VIX-ratio rule traded SVXY, which has run at half the leverage since February 2018, in place of the delisted XIV. The style-momentum page's text and code disagree on sign, and the text was taken as the published intent. In round 2, CTREND ran on Binance spot USDT pairs including delisted ones (685 coins after excluding stable, fiat and leveraged tokens) instead of CoinMarketCap, with trailing 30-day dollar volume replacing market cap as the size proxy, 0.5 gross per leg instead of 1.0, a 20 bp round-trip cost, and a short leg assumed borrowable at zero cost as in the paper. The FOMC rule used SPY with zero-yield cash instead of the Ken French market return. The crypto trend core used for G8 was rebuilt from MEXC 8-hour prices with Binance funding, because MEXC funding history only starts in 2025-04. So "as published" means "as published, on the nearest free data", and a failure could in principle reflect a substitution rather than the idea. Round 1 also relied partly on secondary summaries (CXO Advisory, Quantpedia) because the SSRN papers returned 403; for those candidates the code follows the summary's rules.

The funnel before testing was steep. Round 1 collected 22 candidates and 7 passed the eligibility screen; round 2 produced 2 eligible candidates from about 10 collected; the round-3 research found none worth registering. (The config files list 16 and 8 screened-out entries, some bundling several ideas, so the counts do not reconcile exactly.) Roughly a quarter to a third of collected ideas were testable at all, mostly because published rules omit a parameter, give two conflicting definitions, depend on discontinued or paid data, or need intraday timing.

## Allocation returns were mostly exposure, and the deflated-Sharpe hurdle did the rest

The allocation models look respectable until they meet their own structure. VAA-G12 earned a development Sharpe of 0.86 with t of 2.81, yet random books holding the same weight vectors on randomly permuted assets averaged a daily mean of 0.00023 against its 0.00045, so about half the return was structure, and the empirical p was 0.099. GEM's null mean was 0.00032 against 0.00047 (p 0.105). PAA2's null earned 0.00023 against 0.00026 (p 0.49), and the oil rule's random timing earned 0.00052 against 0.00050 (p 0.62), so the oil rule's return is entirely time spent in SPY. The development windows were long equity and bond bull periods, and the null reproduces that exposure for free. VAA's authors report a Sharpe that fell from 1.24 in-sample to 0.51 out of sample in their own split ([CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=30082)), and our validation Sharpe of 0.51 landed on that figure. The next point is inference, not a ledger result: monthly decisions across 8 to 9 development years give about 100 decisions, too few to separate such a rule from its exposure at p of 0.05, so the G4 failures are partly a power limit and not proof of no skill.

G5 then decided every case where the other gates were marginal. Its hurdle is the expected maximum Sharpe among N skill-free trials, which grows with the number of trials and with the dispersion of their Sharpe ratios. In the ledger it was 0.082 in daily units after round 1 (N of 7, trial standard deviation 0.0296), about 1.30 annualized, and 0.1164 after round 2 (N of 9, standard deviation 0.0618), about 1.85 annualized. The jump is mostly dispersion: CTREND's -0.1275 daily Sharpe roughly doubled the standard deviation. A search that tests diverse ideas pays for the diversity, whether or not the losers are informative. The repo's helper centres the luck benchmark on the trials' mean Sharpe (0.041 after round 1, 0.022 after round 2), which is stricter than the textbook version in [Bailey and López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551), where the expected maximum is measured from zero. My recomputation from the ledger's N and dispersion (not a ledger field) puts the textbook hurdle at about 0.041 daily (0.65 annualized) after round 1 and about 0.094 daily (1.49 annualized) at N of 9. That textbook hurdle still exceeds the best development Sharpe of any of the nine (0.072 daily for the VIX-ratio rule, which failed G3, G4 and G6 anyway), so no candidate would have passed even on the textbook version at the final trial count. At the round-1 count of 7, four of the seven point Sharpes sat above the textbook hurdle, but the gate needs a 0.95 probability, and my rough standard-error arithmetic puts those probabilities near 0.7 to 0.9, so the round-1 verdicts do not change either. A fair criticism of G5 is that N counts trials, not independent trials; VAA, PAA and GEM are closely related, so the effective count is lower. That would soften the hurdle, but nowhere near enough: a candidate needs roughly a 1.5 Sharpe over a decade-long sample.

Three results deserve individual reading. Turn-of-month was the near-miss: it passed seven gates, but each marginally (t exactly 2.00, validation Sharpe 0.41 against 0.40, null p 0.048, cost wall 46 against 30 bps required) and failed G5 with a DSR probability of 0.004, because 17% time in the market produces a daily Sharpe of 0.034 against a 0.082 hurdle. Its validation Sharpe of 0.41 has t of 1.18, and at that rate reaching t of 2 would take about 23 years (t grows like Sharpe times the square root of years; my arithmetic). That fits earlier findings that the effect disappeared after 1990 in futures ([Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research/publications/wp/2000/11.aspx)) and is concentrated on the first day of the month in the ETF era ([Financial Planning Association](https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/article/journal/APR11-turn-month-anomaly-age-etfs-reexamination-return-enhancement-strategies)).

The FOMC-cycle rule passed the timing null at p of 0.003 and replicated part of its paper (development Sharpe 0.67 against the paper's 0.83 gross, [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=28978)), then collapsed to a validation Sharpe of 0.19 (t 0.56). It also correlated 0.74 with SPY, over the 0.7 limit, which is unsurprising for a long-SPY rule invested about half the time. Its 2020-2026 failure agrees with the decay found for the related pre-FOMC drift.

CTREND is the instructive case. The paper reports a gross long-short Sharpe of 1.94 for 2015 to 2022, 3.87% a week gross and 2.90% net of 30/40 bp costs ([JFQA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109024000747)). Our implementation, built from the paper's text and online supplement (28 indicators, 52-week rolling window, elastic net with corrected AIC), lost money in its development window of 2020-10-26 to 2022-05-31 (583 days, Sharpe -2.44, t -2.60, maximum drawdown 45%), with a G4 p-value of 0.999: the real book earned a daily mean of -0.00093 where random books on the same coins averaged zero. All eight parameter perturbations were also negative or flat in that window (Sharpe from -2.5 to +0.01). The window ends where the paper's sample ends, so the paper's performance should have appeared here, although it covers only the last 1.6 years of a sample dominated by earlier data. From 2022-06 to 2026-09 the same code earned a Sharpe of 0.95 (t 1.91, cumulative +123%, maximum drawdown 32%), so the strategy is positive after publication but not in the window the protocol designated as development. That post-2022 number is also cost-fragile: at the paper's own 30/40 bp costs the validation Sharpe falls to 0.28, and at 40/50 bp to 0.02. The elastic net also kept an average of 2.7 indicators per week against about 15 in the paper, and 112 of 422 weeks had no kept forecast. We cannot tell whether that sparsity reflects a real post-2022 change in the cross-section, our substitutions (volume instead of market-cap weights, one trailing window used for both stages), or an implementation difference; the paper never states its lambda grid, week boundary or risk-free rate, so some choices were ours. The result is therefore not a refutation of CTREND. It says that this implementation, on this data, did not reproduce the paper when the protocol said it should have. Cakici and coauthors also report that machine-learning crypto alphas concentrate in small, illiquid coins ([IRFA 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001765.html)), which a $1 million dollar-volume filter does little to remove.

## The search stopped after round 3 because the ceiling sits below the hurdle

Round 3 asked a narrow question: do any published, ETF-testable strategies reach a long-sample net Sharpe of 1.5 or more with low equity correlation? The answer was no. Lou, Yan and Zhang's Treasury auction-cycle trade has a published net Sharpe of 0.84 in 1998-2008 only, is unprofitable for short windows after costs, and needs on-the-run notes. Hartley and Schwarz's month-end Treasury effect has a Sharpe of about 1 and a sample that ends in 2018. Savor and Wilson's macro-announcement effect is long equity on about 11% of days; its quoted Sharpe near 1.6 is a per-day ratio scaled by the square root of 252, and my arithmetic puts the tradable annual figure nearer 0.6 to 0.7. Alternative-risk-premia indices reach high Sharpe only through many strategies, futures and leverage. The round-3 notes judge the honest ceiling for these ideas at about 0.5 to 1.0 net Sharpe, below the 1.5 to 1.9 deflated-Sharpe hurdle.

Continuing would only have spent trials. Nothing in the protocol lets a candidate pass without clearing G5 at the cumulative count, and every extra trial raises the hurdle. As an illustration only, holding the current dispersion fixed at N of 40, the textbook expected maximum is about 0.135 daily, roughly 2.1 annualized, and the repo version about 0.157, roughly 2.5. The protocol says a spent budget or exhausted rounds produces FAIL, "not extended or rerun with looser gates", and loosening G5 or G4 now would turn every near-miss into a pass by construction. Round 3 was therefore research-only: nothing was registered, no trial was spent, and the ledger stands at 9 of 40.

## What the search learned, and the options that remain

The search learned four things beyond the verdict. First, the pipeline works: eight gates ran on nine very different strategies with no truncation failures, and each gate caught a different problem (G4 and G8 exposure, G3 decay, G6 the VIX-ratio rule's threshold sensitivity, G5 the multiplicity). Second, published net Sharpe ratios for free-data ETF and calendar ideas are 0.5 to 1.0, which cannot clear a 9-trial hurdle of about 1.5 to 1.85, so this gate is a tool for rejecting a zoo, not for choosing among modest edges. Third, SPY timing rules decayed out of sample, both here (validation Sharpe 0.19 for FOMC, 0.41 for turn-of-month, 0.31 for the VIX ratio) and in the literature. Fourth, the earlier research in this project gave the same calibration: a net Sharpe of 0.8 to 1.5 is the realistic band for trend after costs, no audited third-party record backs any bot family, and the creator videos examined contained no verified edge, only overfit loops. The best published crypto trend result, a net Sharpe above 1.5 from [Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends), is a backtest and an upper bound. A web search for published strategies reproduced that picture under fixed gates.

The user has three honest options, none of which authorizes live trading, promotion or filters. The first is to register near-misses as descriptive forward watches, pre-registered from today with the rule, costs, daily-P&L scoring and a fixed end date, so that no part of the 2020-2026 window counts as evidence. Turn-of-month is the clean candidate, but it is mostly SPY exposure on 17% of days (correlation 0.42 with SPY), and its validation Sharpe of 0.41 implies decades to reach t of 2, so a watch would calibrate rather than decide. CTREND's post-2022 behaviour is a second candidate with a faster answer (about 4 years at a Sharpe of 0.95, by the same arithmetic), but it sits inside the crypto factor, which the repo's evidence-rate rule discourages, its shorts need borrow or perpetuals that were assumed free, and its Sharpe moves from 0.95 to 0.28 across the paper's own cost range, so costs must be fixed first. The second option is a new, separately pre-registered search with fewer, higher-prior candidates, labelled a new search and not a continuation. The hurdle falls with fewer trials and lower dispersion; my illustrative textbook figure is about 0.4 annualized for N of 3 at the round-1 dispersion, against 1.5 for N of 9. The risk is accounting honesty: the same researcher, data and validation window carry the 9 trials already spent, so the new protocol should state in advance whether they count, and the safe choice is that they do. The round-3 notes' cleanest lead, month-end Treasuries on SHY/IEF/TLT, adds breadth outside equities, although its ETF form is a different trade from the paper's and the notes frame it as low-expectation. The third option is to stop here, keep the FAIL verdict as the answer, and spend the effort on the independent-breadth work the repo already ranks first, such as the pre-registered cross-asset trend universe.

## Two reviews per run caught real bugs before the single look

The single-look rule means a bug found after a run cannot be fixed without a new trial, so each round's code was reviewed independently before it ran, and the reviews changed results. The logged fixes include the allocation null, which originally redrew a random asset assignment every day and now draws one per rebalance spell, held until the weights change; a mislabelled reference series in the independence gate, which came from the run's review log and which I did not independently verify; the FOMC calendar parser, which mishandled meetings that straddle two months, so 1995-02-01, 1998-07-01 and 2012-08-01 are now the decision days; and CTREND's accounting, which now holds each week buy-and-hold and charges turnover once at the start of the week, with relisted tickers split into separate histories and the null built on the same compounded spells. All were decided before the data were seen. The commit history records that the round-2 code was reviewed twice before its single run.

The papers had to be read from saved PDFs. The fetch tool returned the CTREND, FOMC-cycle and Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu PDFs as compressed binary, SSRN and ScienceDirect refused access, and the first round-2 notes carried only abstracts and snippets, with the CTREND indicator list, window and week boundary marked unverified. The 28 indicator definitions and the 52-week window came from the online supplement, found by following the article page's link, and the notes preserve its printed typos (the moving-average denominator, the EMA smoothing of 1/(1+L), the commodity-channel-index deviation) as choices made by judgement. That is the main reason a CTREND failure is not a clean refutation.

## Conclusion

The search's value is the denominator. Of about 32 web-published candidates, nine could be tested as published and none came close on all gates; the one that failed only on multiplicity did so with a deflated-Sharpe probability of 0.004 against the 0.95 required. That calibrates the repo's own pipeline: a modest, honest edge of Sharpe 0.5 to 1.0 will look like turn-of-month, passing most gates marginally and failing the one that prices the search. A gate that rejects such an edge is working as designed, but it also means this protocol cannot discover one. It can only reject a zoo, so a search that expects to succeed must be small and must start from a stronger prior than a web-published rule.

The CTREND split deserves the most follow-up and the least confidence. A factor that lost 45% in the window where its paper's sample ends and earned a Sharpe of 0.95 afterwards is a fact about regimes and implementation that the data cannot yet separate. Only a forward watch with pre-declared costs and a fixed horizon can, and it will take years, as will any answer about calendar effects whose validation Sharpe sits at 0.2 to 0.4.
