# Public bot evidence confirms our validation lead, exposes fill gaps

**Bottom line: outside YouTube, no open-source framework, community strategy, commercial bot, copy-trading platform or forecasting contest gave us an audited, after-cost, third-party forward record for any strategy family we run.** What the wider ecosystem does offer is (a) a calibration band for trend after costs (net Sharpe about 0.8 to 1.5 live, with 20% drawdowns and multi-year flat spells normal), (b) a short list of validation and fill-realism techniques we do not yet have, and (c) one free, fast, independent dataset, Hyperliquid vault histories. Our pipeline already exceeds the framework norm on multiple-testing control: no framework in the surveyed set implements Deflated Sharpe or PBO natively, and we have both, plus a trial ledger, freeze manifest and review methods. Our weakest spots are mechanical. The paper account uses a flat 5 bps slippage plus a 2 bps fee floor and has no size or capacity dependence. There is no generic truncation-invariance test for causality. Nothing yet stress-tests fill assumptions as a scenario axis. Our ~1 Sharpe blend on consistent venues is a normal professional-grade number, which implies that **forward results of t below 2 for the next three years are the expected outcome, not a failure signal**. The highest-value new study is a pre-registered Hyperliquid vault dispersion and persistence test, roughly 2 to 3 days of work. It adds independent information and can start accumulating survivorship-safe snapshots immediately. The grid, DCA, dip-buy, martingale and copy-trading families should stay rejected.

## What realistic after-cost performance looks like for our families

The best available evidence is academic and institutional rather than from bots. For time-series trend on liquid coins, Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon report a net-of-fees Sharpe above 1.5 for an ensemble of Donchian channels with volatility sizing on a rotating top-20 universe, with 10.8% annualized alpha over Bitcoin ([Barbon](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends)). That is a backtest, with no live record and no drawdown in the abstract, so it is an upper bound. A simpler, single-author trend-sleeve backtest (BTC versus cash, 15 bps per round trip, 2021 to Mar 2026) shows Sharpe 0.84 against 0.71 for static BTC, reported secondhand ([Binance Square repost](https://www.binance.com/de/square/post/352480327447329)). Preprint Sharpes of 2 to 3 on short samples, such as 3.02 over Jan 2023 to Aug 2025, are most plausibly small-sample or overfit ([arXiv 2511.13239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13239)). Our own trend-heavy blend sits at about 1.07 net on the 30-sleeve combination, which falls inside a defensible **0.8 to 1.5 band**. Traditional CTAs set the drawdown prior: the SG Trend Index fell 20.4% from May 2024 to May 2025, and has had 16 drawdowns above 10% since 2000, about one every 18 to 24 months ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)).

The statistics of detection make this a calibration for how we judge forward results. Our own first-principles note puts the forward days to reach t = 2 at about 1,275 for the combined portfolio and 2,100 to 2,600 for each single strategy (`docs/FIRST_PRINCIPLES_EVIDENCE_RATE_2026_09_26.md`). The cross-sectional literature adds that momentum is costly in large coins, draws much of its alpha from the short leg, and fades and concentrates in bull markets ([Fieberg et al. abstract](https://open.icm.edu.pl/handle/123456789/25544)). Momentum crashes can be cut by volatility management and results can hinge on a single coin ([Grobys et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). Our fall in cross-sectional Sharpe from 2.09 to about 1 matches that pattern, though that is an inference and not a test. The practical rule for forward reviews is to judge on three things. First, whether the path stays inside a pre-declared drawdown envelope near 20%. Second, whether the paper account tracks the backtest of the same window within cost tolerance. Third, whether the interval from the existing cluster bootstrap excludes a negative mean at the frozen horizon. A sub-1 forward Sharpe over 6 to 12 months is not informative either way.

Funding and basis offer a clean cautionary parallel. BIS finds crypto carry averaging above 10% a year but driven by retail leverage demand, with high carry predicting crashes and cash-and-carry positions liquidated in drawdowns ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf)). CME one-month basis compressed from about 17% to roughly 4.7 to 5% by early 2026, about the risk-free rate ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/01/22/wall-street-pulls-back-bitcoin-trade)). Ethena's sUSDe yield fell from about 8% to about 3.5% after the 10 Oct 2025 liquidation event ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/newsletter/the-breakdown/issue/post_999b16ee-0fe0-4631-a1d0-2aa15a1d18a7)). Hummingbot ships a cross-exchange funding arbitrage script with no published P&L ([Hummingbot scripts](https://hummingbot.org/scripts/examples)), so our venue-mismatch finding has independent support in structure, if not in a replicated test. A consistent-venue carry expectation of cash plus low single digits, with a fat left tail, is the only defensible prior.

## What the frameworks do that we do not, and what we already do better

The frameworks split cleanly into execution realism and statistical rigor, and they are strong at the first and weak at the second. Freqtrade's lookahead-analysis is a black-box differential test. It re-backtests on truncated data and flags any signal or indicator value that changes, and the recursive-analysis tool varies the warm-up length and compares the last row of each indicator ([freqtrade lookahead-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/), [recursive-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis/)). Both are cheap to port as unit tests. In our tests, a prefix-invariance test exists only for the LSK regime state (`tests/test_lsk_conditional_regime.py`) and a causal-beta test for the portfolio forward module. I found no generic truncation-invariance harness covering the DON8, EMA8, VOL8 and blend feature functions, so that is a real gap, though a narrow one. Freqtrade hyperopt itself has no walk-forward or holdout, and its own docs caution that p-values assume iid trades and that testing many strategies yields low p-values by chance ([freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). Passivbot's optimizer docs state that its screening "does not create an independent holdout test" ([Passivbot optimizing.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enarjord/passivbot/master/docs/optimizing.md)). Against that baseline, our `discovery_v2_evaluation.py` (deflated Sharpe, CSCV PBO, White-style reality check), `robustness.py` (cluster bootstrap, minimum detectable effect, concentration, negative controls), `trial_ledger.py`, the frozen-hash manifest and `review_methods.py` (action-matched null, BH family, masking, single look, anti-gaming flags) are **already present** and beyond what the frameworks ship. Walk-forward embargo between discovery, validation and holdout is also already present in `research_protocol.py`. The one thing the tooling lacks from vectorbt PRO is purging and embargo inside cross-validation folds. That matters little here because our protocol uses fixed pre-registered splits rather than k-fold selection, so I rank it low.

On fills and costs the picture reverses. NautilusTrader offers the richest model: fill models with probabilistic limit fills and slippage, queue-position fills from trade ticks, liquidity consumption and a latency model ([Nautilus fill models](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/nightly/concepts/backtesting/fill-models/), [trade execution](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/backtesting/trade-execution/)). Passivbot requires price to trade through a limit by a buffer (for example 15 bps) and applies market-order slippage plus taker fee ([Passivbot backtesting.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enarjord/passivbot/master/docs/backtesting.md)). Hummingbot's docs say not to trust absolute P&L in bar-based backtests ([Hummingbot backtesting](https://condor.hummingbot.org/bots/backtesting)). Our paper-account replay (`paper_account.py`) fills at the 08:00 open of the decision day, charges max(taker fee, 2 bps) plus a flat 5 bps slippage, rounds to contracts, and replays funding from the MEXC funding series. That is already honest for daily-rebalanced majors at small size. It has no notional-dependent impact, no scenario axis for slippage, and no capacity estimate. `canonical_v3.py` carries a per-turnover slippage parameter and `human_execution.py` a 5 bps default, so a cost-sensitivity grid is a small addition on top of existing parameters. Robustness already has a cost surface and break-even ratio, so the missing piece is applying it to the paper account and adding a size-dependent term.

## Public track records are mostly claims, which is why Hyperliquid vaults stand out

Every other public source failed the verifiability test. NostalgiaForInfinity publishes no performance figures, points to commit comments for backtests, ships an auto-updater that can change the live strategy on a cron schedule, and includes grind and rebuy averaging-down logic ([NFI repo](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity), [release v17.4.379](https://newreleases.io/project/github/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity/release/v17.4.379)). The freqtrade-strategies repo labels its content as educational ([freqtrade-strategies](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade-strategies)). The strat.ninja forward-test aggregator was unreachable, so forward-versus-backtest data from it is a gap, not a finding. Passivbot is an explicitly contrarian market maker that "does not predict prices or follow trends" and has no published results. The only numbers are unverified anecdotes relayed by a competitor, such as about 6.3% annualized over 100 days against a BTC hold of about 16% ([Passivbot](https://github.com/enarjord/passivbot), [Gainium review](https://gainium.io/review/passivbot)). For grids, a 2025 paper finds that under simple assumptions a traditional grid's expected return is essentially zero ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)), and a 6-month Gate.io account came out roughly flat after fees with 5x leverage as a liquidation risk ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/joe_hans_6c082d6c1caf189f/i-ran-a-gateio-btc-grid-bot-for-6-months-real-returns-and-3-fatal-mistakes-2i1p)). The best-documented commercial failure is a platform one: the Dec 2022 3Commas API-key leak, with loss estimates from $14.8M to over $27M depending on source ([Halborn](https://halborn.com/explained-the-3commas-breach-december-2022/)). Copy-trading numbers are marketing: a KuCoin blog claims 97% of leaders were profitable but only 43.61% of copy outcomes were, with the underlying study unlocated ([KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026)). The forecasting contests show the right design but no numbers we can verify. G-Research scored teams on about three months of live data after the deadline, the top three used LightGBM, and the organizers noted "many big jumps and precipitous drops" ([G-Research](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)). Private scores of order 0.01 to 0.03 appear only in an unverified aggregator and need checking on Kaggle before use.

Hyperliquid vaults are the exception, because the ledger is on-chain and free. The documented `vaultDetails` endpoint returns per-vault account-value and PnL history for day, week, month and all-time windows, and a bulk snapshot of all vaults is available at a stats-data host ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.hexdocs.pm/Hyperliquid.Api.Stats.Vaults.html), [info endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint)). Hyperliquid's docs do not define the `apr` field, and the all-vaults list endpoint is not in the official page I fetched, so the schema must be verified by a first call. Third-party sources report vaults with severe dispersion, such as one with a -29.6% one-year CAGR and a -43.9% drawdown and another over 95% below peak and deprecated ([tradingstrategy.ai 100x](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/100x), [Liquidator](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/liquidator)). The same aggregator's snapshots of HLP disagree with each other, and it nets deposit flows, which confirms that returns must be rebuilt from raw equity and PnL series ([HLP page](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/hyperliquidity-provider-hlp)). No published cross-sectional persistence study turned up, so the result would be new. It measures other operators' skill rather than our trend core, so its role is calibration and breadth: it shows how often a visible live track record of a given length predicts the next period, which is exactly the question behind our own forward-review gates. Survivorship bias grows with delay because closed vaults can drop off listings, so snapshotting should begin before the analysis is designed.

## Ranked improvements for our pipeline

The ranking weights evidence quality gained per day of effort, and every item respects the evidence-rate rule: none adds a trend variant on DON8, EMA8, VOL8 or the same 10 coins, none adds per-trade cell diagnostics, and none touches frozen modules. Existing frozen files, canonical accounting and `config/frozen_manifest_v1.json` stay as they are, so the new work goes in new files or versioned successors.

| Rank | What to build | Repo module touched | Effort | Why it raises evidence quality or shortens time to answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hyperliquid vault snapshot collector: hourly or daily timestamped pull of the bulk vault list and `vaultDetails` equity/PnL series into append-only files, hash-pinned like other collectors. Start immediately and pre-register the study later. | new `hyperliquid_vaults.py` beside `hyperliquid_history.py` (already has a `_post` helper and candle/funding fetchers); a scheduled workflow | 0.5 to 1 day | Cheapest independent information available. Survivorship protection accrues only from the day collection starts, so delay has a direct cost. |

It also sets the base rate for how far a track record that looks like Sharpe 1 can be trusted. Not a trend test, so weight it as calibration. |

## Reject list

| Family | Reason |
|---|---|
| Grid, DCA, martingale and Passivbot-style contrarian averaging | Short-volatility payoff. A traditional grid has near-zero expected return under simple assumptions ([arXiv 2506.11921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)), and the leverage case adds liquidation risk. The only quantified results are anecdotes, and the family cannot be tested within the evidence-rate rule. |
| NostalgiaForInfinity-style stacked-indicator dip buying with rebuy | No published performance, a moving target through auto-updates, and averaging-down logic that hides left-tail losses in a high win rate. Many entry conditions would also spend multiple-testing budget on the same coins. |
| Copy-trading and leaderboard following | The only numbers are exchange-marketing claims with an unlocated source, leader PnL and follower PnL diverge, and free historical data with delisted traders does not exist. The one academic lab experiment shows copying raises risk-taking and says nothing about leader skill ([Management Science 2020](https://bse.eu/research/publications/copy-trading)). |
| Market-making (Hummingbot Avellaneda-Stoikov, HLP replication) | Needs queue, latency and adverse-selection data we do not collect, the docs make no profitability claim, and a 2022 incentive campaign anecdote suggests rewards, not spread, drove most profits ([Firo forum](https://forum.firo.org/t/2022-firo-hummingbot-campaign/2290?page=2)). |
| Commercial bot platforms (3Commas, Pionex, Bitsgap, Cryptohopper) | No independent return measurements, only reviews from competitors and aggregators, plus documented key-custody risk. Irrelevant to an order-free repo. |
| Funding and basis carry as a standalone sleeve | Already falsified on consistent venues; the BIS and CME evidence on compression and crash risk agrees. |
| Short-horizon ML forecasting (Kaggle-style, foundation models) | Our zero-shot Kronos test failed (rank IC about 0), and the contest evidence is qualitative with any winning correlations tiny and unverified. |

## Conclusion

The largest lesson from the wider ecosystem is negative and useful: public bot performance is almost never verifiable, so our strict pre-registration is rare rather than excessive, and the sensible response is to import mechanics rather than strategies. The mechanics worth importing are all about making a small, slow-to-confirm edge less fragile: scenario-based fill stress, a capacity estimate, a generic causality gate and a paper-versus-backtest reconciliation. None of them needs new signals on the coins we have already inspected.

The one strategic addition is a change in what counts as independent evidence. A vault dispersion and persistence study cannot tell us whether trend works, but it can tell us, within weeks of data, how much weight any live record of our own length deserves, which sets our review expectations before the paper account matures. Start the snapshot collector first, because it is the only item whose value decays with each day of delay. Open questions that this research could not close: strat.ninja forward-versus-backtest data, G-Research private scores, the exact schema of the bulk vault endpoint, Barbon's drawdown and cost figures, and any post-2021 replication of CTREND. All of them need a primary-source fetch before being relied on.
