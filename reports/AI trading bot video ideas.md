# Trading-bot videos sell hypotheses, not edges

Creator content that claims automated or AI trading bots (Moon Dev's channel, Polymarket bot posts, "I gave an LLM $X" experiments) contains **no verified, after-cost edge**, but it does contain a few testable data ideas that fit this repo. Moon Dev's own committed backtests show **1 of 69 completed runs profitable and none beating buy-and-hold** ([execution results](https://github.com/yolojewjitsu/moon-dev-ai-agents/tree/main/src/data/rbi_v2/07_24_2025/execution_results)). The LLM-trader tournaments (Alpha Arena, StockBench, LiveTradeBench) are single short paths that cannot separate skill from luck, and the one study that deflates by trial count rejects every LLM-discovered strategy ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). The most honest creator material is the small Polymarket READMEs that publish sample sizes and failures, and the most useful free-data material comes from academic and exchange documentation rather than from the videos. After filtering against the repo's constraints (pre-registration, free data, no order placement, evidence-rate rule, and the tested-and-failed list), six ideas survive. In rank order: a Binance open-interest/long-short backfill aimed at volatility and as a historical proxy for the liquidation-flow watch; a screen of Hyperliquid's non-crypto perpetuals as independent breadth; an LLM headline classifier on the existing news-RSS ledger (registration deadline 2026-11-01); a DVOL variance-premium check; a Polymarket crypto-threshold calibration check; and a stablecoin-supply regime variable. The rest, including strategy zoos, LLM buy/sell agents, five-minute Polymarket bots, funding-arbitrage bots, copy-trading and whale-following, are rejected. One limit applies throughout: no YouTube transcripts could be retrieved, so "I Let 8 AI Trading Bots Trade for Me All Day" ([2026-10-06, 1.5K views](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2ugYN8eSeY)) and the other video claims are known only from titles, dates and view counts.

## What the content claims and what can be checked

Moon Dev's channel posts roughly daily and funnels viewers to a live Zoom and a paid package listed at $1,500 lifetime ([sales page](https://moondev.com/go)). Titles carry the strong claims: "Found 44,975%" ([2026-09-08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nroZBpr30vA)), "Test 12,000 Trading Bots in One Day" ([2026-10-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jApbmBW_HH8)), "58% Win Rate" on a Polymarket five-minute bot ([2026-09-17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-7EuAhY1q8)), and "Let AI Actually Trade for Me for 60 Days" ([2026-09-30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RZhiYZFyDx0)). The video descriptions are identical sales-funnel text with no metrics, and the sales page itself makes no verified performance claims, so the test design behind each title is unknown. The reachable evidence is the code. His 2025 agent repo (now 404 on GitHub; read through mirrors) wraps hard-coded thresholds on liquidation, open-interest and funding feeds around an LLM that answers BUY/SELL/NOTHING, with no backtest behind the rule ([liquidation_agent.py](https://github.com/yolojewjitsu/moon-dev-ai-agents/blob/main/src/agents/liquidation_agent.py), [whale_agent.py](https://github.com/yolojewjitsu/moon-dev-ai-agents/blob/main/src/agents/whale_agent.py)). The strategy-generation pipeline ("RBI") runs every backtest on one file, BTC 15-minute bars from 2023-01-01 to 2023-11-20, a year in which buy-and-hold returned **+127.8%** ([backtest agent](https://github.com/brenwhyte/moon-dev-ai-agents/blob/main/src/agents/rbi_agent_pp_multi.py)). Its optimiser is told to hit a 50% target return, the "winning" strategies were engineered for Sharpe 2.0 and over 100 trades, and the same strategy appears with 125 trades at Sharpe 1.84 in one report and 349 trades at Sharpe 2.14 in another ([FINAL_RESULTS.md](https://github.com/yolojewjitsu/moon-dev-ai-agents/blob/main/src/data/rbi/FINAL_WINNING_STRATEGIES/FINAL_RESULTS.md)). No walk-forward, Monte Carlo or deflation code exists; those terms appear only in LLM-written bullet lists. With 1,300-plus generated variants and up to ten optimisation passes each, all selected on the same 11 months, the trial count is large and unrecorded, which is exactly the strategy-zoo failure mode this repo has already documented.

The 2026 Polymarket "5-minute bots" are better documented and more candid. The READMEs grade signals against real candles (liquidations 58.8% directional on 187 signals; CVD divergence 51.4% on 704; MACD 52.4% on 548), disclose that stakes are $5 to $15 and that he has "not cracked" the market, and warn that the family "already failed live once" ([liq_cascade_chaser](https://github.com/moondevonyt/Polymarket-Trading-Bot-Examples-By-Moon-Dev/blob/main/5_minute_bots/liq_cascade_chaser/README.md), [5_minute_bots](https://github.com/moondevonyt/Polymarket-Trading-Bot-Examples-By-Moon-Dev/blob/main/5_minute_bots/README.md)). Treat the numbers with care. A 58.8% hit rate on 187 pooled signals has a 95% interval of roughly 52% to 66% (researcher-computed in the notes), the "95% continuation" figure conditions on a move already under way inside the window and is likely close to mechanical, and the fade strategy's 54.3% win rate on 8,802 windows uses a 4-streak and 3x-ATR filter chosen on the same 52 weeks, which the author says "two cents of slippage cuts ... in half" ([streak_snapper](https://github.com/moondevonyt/Polymarket-Trading-Bot-Examples-By-Moon-Dev/blob/main/5_minute_bots/streak_snapper/README.md)). The only liquidation-based claim with any pull for us is that one-sided liquidation bursts continue in the same direction; it is a five-minute claim on binary contracts, while the repo's registered test is daily and in perpetual-swap returns.

Beyond Moon Dev, the verifiable items are the ones whose authors disclaim profit. A live Polymarket momentum bot lost money (104 trades, profit factor 0.58, Sharpe -2.46) ([falsifylab](https://falsifylab.substack.com/p/strategy-deep-dive-polymarket)), the leading open-source market maker warns it "can lose money" ([poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)), and the viral "$63 to $131,000" stories rest on an X post and a screenshot with no audit ([Elite Trader](https://elitetrader.com/et/threads/trading-bot-that-works.388228/latest)). The only independent on-chain study found that in one week of 15-minute crypto markets, takers won 53% of fills yet lost $728,501 while makers gained the same amount, before fees, and that only 36.8% of wallets were profitable ([Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)). Polymarket then added taker fees to those markets in January 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates)). Named-YouTuber coverage beyond Moon Dev is thin: Part Time Larry's only bot-like asset found is a 2021 supertrend repo, and no freqtrade, Jesse or Hummingbot video with checkable results surfaced.

## LLM trading experiments show noise, not skill

Alpha Arena Season 1 gave six frontier models $10,000 each on Hyperliquid perpetuals from 18 October to 3 November 2025. Four of six lost money; Qwen3-Max ended near +22% and DeepSeek near +5% ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). DeepSeek led at +120% nine days in and finished at +4.9%, so rank and sign reversed within a week ([interim](https://forklog.com/en/deepseek-surpasses-gpt-5-in-cryptocurrency-trading-competition/)). The sample is six entrants, one run each, about 16 days and up to roughly 20x leverage, with no passive benchmark; the organiser himself said the early results may be luck, and critics make the same point ([Boris Again](https://borisagain.substack.com/p/why-alpha-arena-is-literally-the)). Secondary sources disagree on final losses for four of the six models, so only the top two returns are reliable. The reverse-engineered prompt fed models standard technical indicators plus open interest and funding ([prompt reconstruction](https://gist.github.com/wquguru/7d268099b8c04b7e5b6ad6fae922ae83)), which the repo already holds; the experiment tests an LLM acting as a leveraged discretionary technical trader, not a new information source.

The academic benchmarks agree. StockBench found most LLM agents fail to beat equal-weight buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis, and rankings flipped between windows ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)). LiveTradeBench found that a model's LMArena rank had a Spearman correlation of 0.054 with stock return and -0.38 with Polymarket return, with no baseline, costs or significance tests ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). FINSABER re-tests published LLM timing results over about 20 years and finds earlier advantages deteriorate significantly ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)). TradingAgents' Sharpe of 5.6 to 8.2 comes from a three-month, three-stock window inside the LLMs' training data, and its authors concede the figure is above their expected range ([TradingAgents](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138)). The Gençay study is the strongest check: with look-ahead blocked and performance deflated by logged trials, every LLM-discovered strategy was rejected across two frontier models and up to 100 candidates ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). This independently supports the repo's Kronos and strategy-zoo failures. The reusable part is narrower: an LLM as an auditable text-to-feature classifier feeding a conventional pre-registered signal, which Lopez-Lira and Tang report as positively related to next-day returns, strongest for small caps and bad news, and decaying as adoption grows (read from a search summary of the paper, not the PDF) ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v5)).

## Free history is deep for open interest and options volatility, absent for liquidations

The data audit decides which ideas can be answered quickly. Binance's public bulk archive has daily `metrics` files with open interest, global and top-trader long/short ratios and a taker buy/sell ratio at five-minute resolution, starting 2020-09-01 for BTCUSDT and 2021-12-01 for SOLUSDT and DOGEUSDT ([metrics file](https://data.binance.vision/data/futures/um/daily/metrics/BTCUSDT/BTCUSDT-metrics-2024-01-15.zip), [bucket listing](https://s3-ap-northeast-1.amazonaws.com/data.binance.vision?delimiter=/&prefix=data/futures/um/daily/metrics/BTCUSDT/&max-keys=3)). The live API serves only the latest 30 days of the same series ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics)). The archive objects show a last-modified date of 2026-03-18, so early rows may be back-filled, and a point-in-time check belongs in any pre-registration. Deribit's DVOL index has 2,008 daily candles back to 2021-03-24 ([DVOL endpoint](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_volatility_index_data?currency=BTC&start_timestamp=1400000000000&end_timestamp=1790000000000&resolution=1D)), and option trades with implied volatility are available from 2022 via the history host ([history.deribit.com](https://history.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_last_trades_by_currency_and_time?currency=BTC&kind=option&start_timestamp=1640995200000&end_timestamp=1641081600000&count=2)). Hyperliquid funding history is paginated at 500 records per call from about 2023-05-12, daily candles reach back years, but hourly candles cover only about six months because the API keeps the latest 5,000 candles ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint), [live probe](https://api.hyperliquid.xyz/info)). DefiLlama's aggregate stablecoin series has 3,236 daily rows from 2017-11-29 ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoincharts/all)).

Liquidations are the exception. No Binance `liquidationSnapshot` prefix exists in the bulk bucket, OKX returns only recent events, and Moon Dev's multi-exchange liquidation archive is paid, frozen for Binance at 2026-04-23 with a permanent gap to 2026-07-18 ([moondev.com/docs](https://moondev.com/docs)). Hyperliquid position and whale history needs a requester-pays S3 archive with no completeness guarantee ([Hyperliquid historical data](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/historical-data)). The repo's forward-only OKX collector (`config/liquidation_flow_v1.json`, start 2026-10-12, daily-horizon hypotheses H1 and H2, review after 180 days) is therefore the correct free instrument, and the Hyperliquid venue check (`config/hyperliquid_venue_check_v1.json`) already covers the Hyperliquid price-and-funding replication that the video review prompted. Neither needs to be repeated; the open question is how to shorten the liquidation watch's 180-day wait.

The predictive literature is thinner than the signals' popularity implies. Carry predicting crashes has a BIS working paper behind it, but the effect is a tail-risk signal and carry profitability compressed after 2024 ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm), [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435) via snippet). That fits the repo's own finding that the funding-carry leg failed with consistent-venue funding. Open-interest studies relate unexpected open interest to volatility, not returns ([IRFA 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v86y2023ics1057521923000133.html), abstract level). No peer-reviewed out-of-sample test of long/short ratios, liquidation reversals, whale positioning or DVOL as a return signal turned up. Seven BTC liquidation cascades showed variance rising coincident with, not before, each event ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070)). Stablecoin evidence conflicts: Griffin and Shams find Tether issuance followed by price rises, while Wei finds no effect and NY Fed work finds flows respond to prices ([Wei](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v171y2018icp19-22.html), [Fed](https://fedinprint.org/item/fednls/99893)). The pattern that repeats is that these variables relate more reliably to volatility than to returns, which argues for a volatility target as the first, fastest test.

## Fit against the repo's rules narrows the field to six ideas

The evidence-rate rule blocks anything that re-slices DON8, EMA8 or VOL8, or the same 10 coins, and anything that adds crypto-only trend variants. Every surviving idea therefore either reaches outside the crypto factor, adds a different information type, or can be answered from existing free history instead of a 180-day forward wait. The backfillable open-interest idea ranks first because it is the only way to get a multi-year, daily-scored answer on liquidation-like events without waiting for the OKX collector. It carries a stated caveat: a large open-interest drop on a large price move is an unvalidated proxy for liquidation flow, and the first step is the point-in-time provenance check. The Hyperliquid non-crypto perpetual screen ranks second because the repo's own evidence-rate document names the next step as a cross-asset universe chosen without looking at strategy P&L and screened only on availability, liquidity and correlation to the crypto factor (`docs/FIRST_PRINCIPLES_EVIDENCE_RATE_2026_09_26.md`). The notes report that Moon Dev's data layer tracks Hyperliquid HIP-3 stock, commodity and FX perpetuals ([README](https://github.com/moondevonyt/Hyperliquid-Data-Layer-API)), and Ethena coverage cites equity-perp funding near 14% on Hyperliquid and 17.5% on Binance ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/), secondary). The history depth of those markets is unverified and likely short, so the screen is a read-only inspection that fixes the universe before any strategy runs.

The LLM headline classifier is third because it adds independent text information on top of infrastructure the repo already runs (the news-RSS ledger, collecting since 2026-09-30), and because the window to register a new version before the N1 to N4 strategies begin scoring on 2026-11-01 is closing. It cannot be backtested, since headlines cannot be recovered point-in-time, and it inherits the literature's warnings about decay and its weak crypto evidence. The remaining three are cheap and descriptive. The DVOL variance-premium check has verified 5.5-year depth but covers only BTC and ETH, so it adds little breadth. The Polymarket crypto-threshold calibration check adds an independent price source, but history depth for resolved markets is unverified and no study tests whether such prices predict returns. The stablecoin-supply variable has the longest history, but it is a single slow series with reverse causality and conflicting findings, which is the profile of the Fear and Greed test that already failed; rank it last and expect the same fate.

## Conclusion

The video genre's lasting contribution is a set of cheap questions, not strategies. Moon Dev's own failed-experiment logs, the falsifylab live loss and the LLM-benchmark reversals all say the same thing: short samples with post-hoc cell selection produce hit rates that look like edges, and the repo's pre-registration discipline already addresses what those creators lack. Two things are new. First, the liquidation question can be given a multi-year proxy answer from free Binance open-interest history instead of a 180-day wait, provided the proxy is validated against the OKX forward data. Second, the repo's breadth problem has a concrete free candidate in non-crypto perpetuals, which the creator content mentions only in passing. Everything else in the genre either repeats a tested-and-failed family or needs paid, latency-sensitive or order-placing infrastructure that the repo excludes.

## Ideas to reject

| Idea | Why it fails the repo's rules |
|---|---|
| Moon Dev "RBI" strategy zoo and the "Karpathy loop" (44,975%, 12,000 bots) | Same family as the failed strategy zoos; one asset, one up-year, untracked trial count, in-sample targets; 1 of 69 profitable and none beat buy-and-hold; the loop claims are title-only and untestable. |
| LLM BUY/SELL agents, Alpha Arena style leaderboards, multi-agent debate (TradingAgents) | No evidence beyond technical features the repo already holds; single short paths; deflated tests reject all LLM-discovered strategies; the Season 1 window also falls outside the 3-minute and hourly candle cap of the free API. |
| Polymarket five-minute bots (liquidation chaser, streak fade, mid-price lag arbitrage, corridor and box makers) | Binary payoffs and taker fees consume a thin edge; the strongest "continuation" statistic is likely mechanical; venue and latency specific; execution requires order placement. |
| Funding-arbitrage bots and vaults (Hummingbot templates, Hyperliquid vaults, Ethena-style carry) | The repo's own carry leg failed with consistent-venue funding; BTC funding and carry Sharpe compressed after 2024; vendor returns are self-reported with 0% fee lines. |
| Copy-trading, whale and "smart money" following, HLP sentiment z-scores | No history (current snapshots only, requester-pays archive), undefined signal formulas, survivorship in wallet selection, and no independent test anywhere. |
| Grid bots | No rigorous crypto study; the one backtest found shows a positive grid profit and a -33% total return. |
| Order-book imbalance, Twitter scraping sentiment, Tether-mint timing | Millisecond horizons dominated by costs and latency; scraping is terms-of-service fragile and Fear and Greed already failed; the issuance effect is contested and tied to a 2017-18 sample. |
| Alpha Arena wallet forensics and a random-trader null | Needs requester-pays S3 or capped fill history, adds no strategy information, and sits in the same crypto factor. |
