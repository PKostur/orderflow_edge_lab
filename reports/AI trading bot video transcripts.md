# Moon Dev's transcripts show loops that overfit

Read in full, Moon Dev's videos contain **no verified edge, no out-of-sample profit and no live record that survives costs**. The most informative content is the creator's own failures. His "Karpathy loop" was scored in sample on ETH 6-hour bars (2017-2022), and his own post-mortem shows the leaderboard ranking inverting out of sample, with out-of-sample return falling from about 75% to about 12% ([video 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rm9arc2t5Xc), 0:08:11-0:08:40). The "12,000 bots" headline is a count of backtest runs of one idea (a time-of-week short) crossed with 19 mostly correlated files, and his own printed counts sum to under 9,000 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jApbmBW_HH8), 0:38:40-0:57:56). The "AI trades for me" videos show six LLMs on $100 accounts finishing between +0.15% and -16% while BTC rose about 33% ([60-day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RZhiYZFyDx0), [48-day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2S2C5MYPXCs)). The "8 AI trading bots trade for me" video shows no trading at all. The one rigorous piece is a null result: about 45,000 LLM calls on equity price bars found no edge against an action-matched null, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction and a ticker/date masking test ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_N15W3X-8rM)). **No strategy idea in the transcripts passes the repo's evidence-rate rule.** What is worth importing is method: an action-matched null, a multiple-testing family, masking and calibration checks for any LLM step, a stop rule against peeking, and a short list of anti-gaming checks. Coverage is 21 of 22 cited videos, all from Moon Dev (details below).

## Coverage, provenance and corrections to the earlier report

Twenty-one of the 22 cited Moon Dev videos were transcribed in full. The 22nd, "i lost $1,986 vibe coding with claude so im quitting & coding by hand again" (HJBbAHfndh0), is age-restricted and returned no transcript. Every transcript is a YouTube auto-caption, so numbers are sometimes garbled; the notes flag the cases (average win and loss in the chat-vote bot, fee amounts, a buy-and-hold figure). Every figure is the creator reading his own screen; no video links a trade file, ledger or wallet that can be checked, so all results are claims. **Other creators' transcripts are not covered.** YouTube rate-limited this IP, so those will be added in a follow-up and nothing below should be read as a verdict on them. I worked from the three transcript-analysis notes (which cite each statement by video and timestamp) plus the earlier notes, and spot-checked two quotes against the raw files; I did not re-read every transcript. Timestamps are `h:mm:ss` as given in the notes.

The transcripts contradict the earlier report in six places. First, the earlier report said the video claims were known only from titles; they are now known from content. Second, it grouped "12,000 bots" with the Karpathy loop as title-only, untestable claims. They are separate. The 12,000 comes from a time-of-week scan, and the loop was run and partly tested out of sample by the creator himself, with a poor result. Third, the loop ran on ETH 6-hour bars, not BTC, so the earlier inference that 44,975% was in sample is confirmed but its asset was wrong. Fourth, the "58% Win Rate" title belongs to a chat-voted bot buying $5 positions, not to the liquidation-based READMEs the earlier report discussed beside it; its own author says the bot lost money, and the win rate is not a profit statistic ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-7EuAhY1q8), 0:06:24, 0:10:05). Fifth, "I Let 8 AI Trading Bots Trade for Me All Day" is a queue of LLM research and backtest workers; no order is placed, and he declines to show profits ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2ugYN8eSeY), 0:36:49-0:38:10). Sixth, the "Let AI Actually Trade for Me for 60 Days" title reads as a success claim, but the content is a negative result with the best model at +0.15%. One unresolved discrepancy remains: the transcripts' internal cues suggest the 12,000-bot and 8-bot videos were recorded in October 2025 (a results file dated "2025 1016", Halloween on a Friday), while the earlier notes list 2026 upload dates. This does not affect any conclusion, but I could not reconcile it.

## Methodology worth importing, and where it lands

The best method content is in the Jev price-bar test, and each of its parts has a place in this repo. I did not inspect the repo's current protocol documents, so "if not already present" applies to each item.

The **action-matched null** scores a signal against the base rate for the same number and direction of actions, rather than against 50%. The video shows why: the "next bar up" rate in an early sample was 38.3%, so an apparent edge was luck ([_N15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_N15W3X-8rM), 0:06:33). It applies to the sparse, event-driven tests: the OKX liquidation-flow hypotheses H1 and H2, the news-sentiment N1-N4 strategies, and the planned headline classifier, each against randomly drawn days with the same count and direction mix. It also applies to the trend core and blend forward watches, where matching on long/short mix and exposure stops beta being credited as skill. The 60-day video's "better at longing" and the Tori Trades "beats buy-and-hold at 27% exposure" are the same error in the wild.

**Benjamini-Hochberg across the pre-registered tests** was applied by him across six tests, where the lowest raw p of .0387 sat far above its .0083 threshold and "nothing survives" ([_N15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_N15W3X-8rM), 0:07:22). It belongs in any pre-registration that lists several hypotheses: a family declared before data, covering for example H1, H2, N1-N4 and any shortlisted descriptive tests. Two cautions from me, not the video: the family must be fixed in advance (otherwise the correction is itself a researcher choice), and it controls the false discovery rate, so confirmatory primary thresholds such as t of 3 should stay as registered.

**Ticker and date masking**, with a named-versus-blind comparison, showed that dating a backtest card inflated the model's recognition score (0.789 versus 0.288 on 97.7% of 14,828 paired windows) while the edge over the null stayed within about 4 points ([_N15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_N15W3X-8rM), 0:08:00). For us it applies to any LLM step that sees retrospective text. A forward-only headline classifier on a pinned model carries less contamination risk, but a named-versus-blind run on the first weeks of the news ledger would show whether labels depend on coin identity beyond the text. His **calibration buckets**, which came out flat and slightly inverted (0:08:45), belong in the same classifier's report; no confidence threshold should be set before the buckets are read. His free **random-forest and logistic baselines** (AUC 0.49 and 0.499) support one rule: give each LLM feature a trivial non-LLM baseline, such as the existing word-list score.

A **stop rule against interim peeking** is demonstrated by his own accident. At 2,396 calls, scaled HOOD showed +10.1% (p = 0.045) and QQQ +21% (p = 0.02); at 535 trades the HOOD result fell to 0.9% ([_N15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_N15W3X-8rM), 0:05:16-0:06:15). That is the argument for fixing the sample size or date before scoring and for keeping interim readouts descriptive in the forward watches, including the 180-day liquidation-flow review. **Anti-gaming checks** come from the loop post-mortems and apply to any scorer or future search tooling: ban size as a lever and test return and Sharpe on identical trades; never rank on the in-sample score; test that a composite score cannot be positive for a losing series (his two-negatives bug, [l0ft](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0fthd71hOE) 0:11:51); use causal rolling thresholds, never full-sample percentiles; and report the cost wall. The simplest defence is to keep the primary metric single and pre-registered, so the composite-score bugs cannot arise.

## Conclusion

The transcripts change one earlier judgement and confirm another. The change: the "Karpathy loop" is not an unknowable headline. It is a documented example of the failure the repo's pre-registration exists to prevent, with the creator's own out-of-sample data showing rank inversion and one-point objective gaming, and with an agent that tells him the gains are leverage (b70o 0:10:43). The confirmation: the hypothesis-generation genre yields cheap questions rather than strategies, and the transcripts add no independent-information idea beyond the earlier shortlist. The one liquidation rule he states failed out of sample in his own test, which is a weak negative prior (single number, no sample size) for the repo's registered OKX test but no reason to change it.

The practical implication is to spend the value on defences rather than ideas. The best returns for our evidence rate are a matched null and a declared multiple-testing family in the next pre-registrations, a stop rule and cost-wall line in the forward-watch reports, and masking and calibration checks before the headline classifier is registered ahead of its 2026-11-01 deadline. The open uncertainty is the rest of the creator field: the follow-up transcripts of other YouTubers may contain better-documented tests than Moon Dev's, and this report cannot speak for them.
