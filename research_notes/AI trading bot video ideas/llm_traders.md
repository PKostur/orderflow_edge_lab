# Public LLM-trades-markets experiments (2024-2026): design, outcomes, verifiability, extractable ideas

Research date: 2026-10-09. All figures below are as reported by the cited pages. Fetch tools returned summarised page text, so numbers were not independently checked against the primary sites. nof1.ai's own pages (blog, leaderboard) were not reachable (404 on the guessed blog URLs; alphaarena.ai was not fetched). Alpha Arena numbers therefore come from secondary write-ups, and these conflict in places (flagged inline).

## 1. Which experiments exist (names, dates, URLs), and were the outcomes statistically meaningful or dominated by leverage/luck?

### Takeaway
Alpha Arena Season 1 (crypto perps, Hyperliquid, 18 Oct to 3 Nov 2025, six frontier LLMs, $10k each, ~16 days, one run per model) is the flagship; its ranking is a single short path with 6 entrants, up to ~20x leverage and an interim leaderboard that reversed several times. Nothing public gives significance tests, confidence intervals, repeated runs or a passive benchmark. The academic benchmarks (StockBench, LiveTradeBench, FINSABER) mostly find LLM agents do not reliably beat buy-and-hold, and results flip between market regimes. The viral "gave ChatGPT $100" stock experiment ended in a loss.

### Cited Findings

**Alpha Arena (nof1.ai), Season 1: crypto perps on Hyperliquid**
- Ran 18 Oct to 3 Nov 2025; six models: DeepSeek V3.1 Chat, Qwen3-Max, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4; each given $10,000 of real capital, identical instructions and market data, trading through Hyperliquid wallets whose trades are publicly viewable — [SCMP via search summary](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3331425/alibabas-ai-model-outperforms-us-rivals-crypto-trading-showdown) (SCMP page itself returned 403; details from the search-tool summary), [ForkLog 4 Nov 2025](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)
- Final balances per ForkLog: Qwen3-Max $12,231 (+22.3%), DeepSeek $10,489 (+4.9%), Claude Sonnet 4.5 $5,799, Gemini 2.5 Pro $5,445, Grok $4,208, GPT-5 $4,126; four of six lost money — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)
- CONFLICT on final losses: iWeaver lists Claude -30.81%, Grok -45.3%, Gemini -56.71%, GPT-5 -62.66% — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/); these do not match ForkLog's balances (e.g. Claude $5,799 would be -42%, GPT-5 $4,126 would be -58.7%). Only the top two (+22.3%, +4.9%) agree across sources. Likely different snapshot times; unresolved.
- Interim leaderboard on 27 Oct 2025 (nine days in): DeepSeek +120% ($22,031), Qwen +80%, Claude +21%, Grok +9%, Gemini -59%, GPT-5 -61.5% — [ForkLog 27 Oct](https://forklog.com/en/deepseek-surpasses-gpt-5-in-cryptocurrency-trading-competition/). By the end DeepSeek had fallen to +4.9% and Claude/Grok had turned deeply negative, so rank and sign reversed within about a week. iWeaver notes DeepSeek peaked well above its start before ending near +4.9%, i.e. a large drawdown hidden by the final return — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)
- Sample size: Qwen3-Max reportedly made about 43 trades (under 3/day) with a 30.2% win rate, per secondary summaries — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/), [search summary of MEXC/GNcrypto/others](https://blog.mexc.com/news/race-against-time-how-ai-models-battled-live-crypto-markets-in-alpha-arena). Trade counts for other models were not found except one claim (below). A 30% win rate with positive P&L means payoff skew carried the result.
- nof1's own founder (Jay Zhang) said the early results may be luck and that models received only quantitative data (no news) — reported in [ForkLog 4 Nov](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/) and the SCMP-derived search summary; the iWeaver review adds that "one path cannot reveal consistency", a single run, six models, one regime — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)
- Critique by Boris Tseitlin: six models, one instance each, ~2 weeks cannot separate skill from luck; a market crash hit everyone; the "benchmark of intelligence" framing is overclaimed; no multiple instances, no buy-and-hold baseline, results not reproducible — [Boris Again (Substack)](https://borisagain.substack.com/p/why-alpha-arena-is-literally-the)
- nof1 stated Season 2 would bring "numerous improvements", including running several prompts in parallel and several variants of each model — [ForkLog 4 Nov](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/). As of a 6 Aug 2026 check by TradeRank (via search summary), no public Season 2 leaderboard was visible on the site; I found no published Season 2 results — [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/alpha-arena-alternatives-2026)

**Alpha Arena Season 1.5: US equities (late Nov to early Dec 2025)**
- Moved to US stocks (reports name TSLA, NVDA, MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, PLTR); about two weeks, four "competitions" with different modes (New Baseline with news and memory, Monk Mode for capital preservation, Situational Awareness showing rivals' rank, Max Leverage forcing high leverage). Winner an initially undisclosed "mystery model", later named as Grok 4.20, averaging +12% across the four competitions and profitable in all four; GPT-5.1 second, Gemini 3 third; no return figures for the others, no starting capital stated — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/), [OneDayAdvisor (secondary, flags several claims as unsourced)](https://www.onedayadvisor.com/2026/01/AI-stock-trading.html)
- Verifiability: organiser says trades and outputs are publicly checkable (ForkLog). The winner was a pre-release model, so the result is not reproducible by outsiders.

**Academic / open benchmarks**
- StockBench (arXiv 2510.02209): 20 top DJIA stocks, 3 Mar to 30 Jun 2025 (82 trading days), $100k start, 13 models, 3 seeds each. Equal-weight buy-and-hold: +0.4% return, -15.2% max drawdown. Best models +2.4/2.5% (Qwen3-235B), GPT-5 +0.3%, GPT-OSS-20B -2.8%. Authors say most agents fail to beat buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted return; in the Jan-Apr downturn no LLM agent beat the baseline; rankings shifted a lot between windows (GPT-OSS-120B went from last to first) — [StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)
- LiveTradeBench (arXiv 2511.03628): 50 live days (18 Aug to 24 Oct 2025), 21 LLMs, 15 US equities/ETFs plus cash and 10 Polymarket binary markets, inputs prices, holdings and Google News titles. Stock cumulative return only ~1.8% to 6.3% across models; Polymarket far more dispersed (Claude-Sonnet-3.7 +20.5%, Qwen3-235B-Thinking -57.6%). Sharpe correlation across the two markets near zero; Spearman correlation of LMArena score with return 0.054 (stocks) and -0.38 (Polymarket). No baseline, significance tests, repeated runs or transaction costs reported — [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)
- FINSABER (arXiv 2505.07078, Li, Kim, Cucuringu, Ma): re-tests published LLM timing strategies over ~20 years and 100+ symbols; earlier LLM advantages "deteriorate significantly"; LLM strategies too conservative in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)
- Gençay (arXiv 2608.27734, 27 Aug 2026): LLM agent searching for strategies on a 453-stock point-in-time US universe and a 39-ETF universe with costs; with look-ahead blocked and performance deflated by the logged trial count, every LLM-discovered strategy was rejected across two frontier models, up to 100 candidates and five repeated runs, while passive benchmarks pass — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)

**Retail / stock "gave ChatGPT money" experiments**
- Nathan Smith's ChatGPT micro-cap portfolio (human executes trades; run 27 Jun to 27 Dec 2025): widely shared +24-25% after ~4 weeks; his final weekly update (26 Jan 2026) put the account at $82.88 from a ~$100 start, max drawdown -50.33% (6 Nov 2025), negative Sharpe/alpha; S&P 500 over the same window about +10%. Figures are self-published, no independent verification; the model is variously described as GPT-4o or ChatGPT 5 — [Benzinga (Aug 2025)](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/25/08/46783783/redditors-100-chatgpt-day-trading-experiment-trounces-two-benchmark-indexes-with-24-returns-here), [Hackernoon weekly series](https://sia.hackernoon.com/can-chatgpt-outperform-the-market-week-26) (final-number summary came from a search tool, not a direct read)
- Rallies.ai AI Trading Arena (2026): GPT-5, Claude, Gemini, Grok etc. with $100k hypothetical portfolios in US stocks; one article says DeepSeek-v3 led with ~+4.6% over one month; no methodology, not audited — [OneDayAdvisor](https://www.onedayadvisor.com/2026/01/AI-stock-trading.html)
- YouTube "I gave AI $X to trade" videos: I could not locate a specific verifiable video with a results table; only a video summary (OpenClaw agent, $100/$1,000/$10,000 challenges, results not confirmed) and unsourced claims (e.g. "ChatGPT lost $7,200 of $10,000 in one week, 2 of 44 trades successful") — [Lilys summary](https://lilys.ai/ar/notes/d5-render-20260219/ai-trades-10k-crypto-while-i-sleep), [Novyny.live](https://novyny.live/en/tehnologii/chatgpt-tried-crypto-trading-and-lost-nearly-all-its-money-290155.html). Treat as unverified.

**Agent frameworks (backtest-only, no live money)**
- TradingAgents (Xiao, Sun, Luo, Wang; arXiv 2412.20138): reports on AAPL/GOOGL/AMZN, 1 Jan to 29 Mar 2024 (about three months): cumulative return 26.6% / 24.4% / 23.2%, Sharpe 8.21 / 6.39 / 5.60, max drawdown 0.9% / 1.7% / 2.1%, versus buy-and-hold -5.2% / +7.8% / +17.1% and rule baselines (MACD, KDJ+RSI, ZMR, SMA). Authors admit the Sharpe exceeds their expected range, attribute it to few pullbacks and cite compute cost as the reason for the short window. Paper text lists different tickers in section 5.1 (Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Google) than in Table 1, and the 2024 window sits inside the training data of the LLMs used — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138)
- virattt/ai-hedge-fund: ~64k GitHub stars (per page fetch), MIT; states it is a proof of concept for education, makes no trades, reports no performance figures; data from Financial Datasets API; paper-trading mode with hash-chained ledger and a backtester, no published results — [GitHub](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund)
- TauricResearch/TradingAgents repo: Apache-2.0, LangGraph; says results are not guaranteed to match published figures and vary run to run; no performance numbers on the page — [GitHub](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)

### Inferences
- With 6 models, 1 run each, ~16 days and up to ~20x leverage, the Alpha Arena ranking is statistically indistinguishable from noise. Interim reversals (DeepSeek +120% to +4.9%; Claude +21% to about -30/-42%) show the path-dependence. A random-trader null with the same leverage would plausibly produce a similar dispersion. Nobody has published that null.
- The outcome spread (-62% to +22%) is consistent with leverage times a short, directional crypto drawdown (a market crash during the window, per Tseitlin) rather than with a skill ordering. The one robust-looking observation is the cross-sectional spread of leverage and trade frequency, not the ranking itself.
- TradingAgents' Sharpe 5-8 on a 3-month, 3-stock window within model training data is a leakage/short-window artefact until shown otherwise; independent work (FINSABER, Gençay, StockBench) points the other way.

### Gaps
- No primary nof1 data: official leaderboard, per-model trade lists, fees and funding totals were not read (nof1.ai blog URLs 404; the Hyperliquid wallet addresses were not located). Per-wallet trade counts, fees and holding times could be computed from public Hyperliquid data but I did not do this.
- Season 1 final loss percentages for models 3-6 conflict between sources (see above).
- Season 2 results (if any) could not be found.
- No published repeat runs of the same model/prompt, no random-trader null and no buy-and-hold BTC/ETH comparison for the Season 1 window were found.
- No verifiable YouTube video with auditable results was found.

## 2. What inputs did the LLMs receive, and what decision rules or behaviours emerged (trend following, over-trading, fee drag)?

### Takeaway
Alpha Arena Season 1 fed models only numeric market data (no news): price, EMA/MACD/RSI/ATR at 3-minute and 4-hour resolution, plus open interest and funding per coin, with a JSON action schema carrying leverage, stop, target and invalidation condition (as reverse-engineered by a third party). Reported behaviours are anecdotal: leverage differences across models, one report of heavy over-trading with fee drag (Gemini), patient low-turnover trading for the winners, and sentiment chasing for Grok. All behaviour claims come from blog write-ups, not audited trade data.

### Cited Findings
- Founder statement: models got only numerical market data ("LLMs do not handle numerical time series data well, but that was the entire context we provided"); action space "rather narrow" — [ForkLog 4 Nov](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)
- Reverse-engineered prompt (author says the official system prompt is not public, so it is an inference): per coin, current price, 20-EMA, MACD, 7-period RSI, open interest (latest vs average) and funding rate; a 3-minute intraday series (mid price, EMA, MACD, RSI-7/14) and 4-hour context (EMA20 vs EMA50, ATR3 vs ATR14, volume, MACD, RSI-14); account state (return %, Sharpe ratio, cash, positions with entry/liquidation price, unrealised P&L, leverage, exit plan). Output JSON: signal (buy_to_enter / sell_to_enter / hold / close), coin, quantity, leverage 1-20, profit target, stop loss, invalidation condition, confidence 0-1, risk_usd, 500-char justification. Rules inferred: market orders only, no pyramiding/hedging/partial exits, at most ~40% of capital per position, leverage bands by conviction (1-3x, 3-8x, 8-20x), reward-to-risk at least 2:1, fees roughly 0.02-0.05% and slippage 0.01-0.1%, decisions every 2-3 minutes — [wquguru gist](https://gist.github.com/wquguru/7d268099b8c04b7e5b6ad6fae922ae83) (reconstruction; the gist itself says the system prompt is guessed and its sizing bands are internally inconsistent)
- Coin universe is reported inconsistently: BTC, ETH, DOGE (ForkLog 27 Oct, SCMP summary) versus six coins BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, DOGE, XRP (gist). Unresolved.
- Reported average leverage by model (one secondary analysis, snapshot time unknown): Grok 4 18.0, Qwen3-Max 15.4, GPT-5 15.0, DeepSeek/Claude/Gemini ~10.0; another summary gives Qwen 16.7x and DeepSeek 12.9x, so the figures conflict — [search summary of leaderboard write-ups](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/alpha-arena-nof1-ai-explained) (the datawallet page itself returned 404; the leverage figures came via a search-tool summary, treat as weak)
- Behaviour claims from secondary sources: Qwen3-Max took fewer trades with longer holds and ~43 trades total; DeepSeek early on was a "patient sniper" (6 trades, >21h average hold; one early snapshot showed $58.51 fees); Gemini 2.5 Pro reportedly made 238 trades (~13/day) and paid ~$1,331 fees (13% of capital) — one source only, interpretation as over-trading is the writer's; Grok 4 reportedly traded on X/Twitter sentiment and held losers too long — [search-summary of MEXC / iWeaver / other write-ups](https://blog.mexc.com/news/race-against-time-how-ai-models-battled-live-crypto-markets-in-alpha-arena)
- Critique of inputs: same large block of precomputed indicators for every model effectively hardcodes one strategy family; no news, tools or reasoning — [Boris Again](https://borisagain.substack.com/p/why-alpha-arena-is-literally-the)
- Season 1.5 modes show nof1 itself treated memory/news, self-knowledge of rank and forced leverage as experimental variables — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)
- TradingAgents inputs: OHLCV, news (Bloomberg, Yahoo, EODHD, FinnHub, Reddit), social sentiment, insider transactions, financial statements, 60 technical indicators; roles: fundamentals/sentiment/news/technical analysts, bull vs bear researcher debate, trader, risk team, portfolio manager; about 11 LLM calls and 20+ tool calls per prediction — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138), [GitHub](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)
- StockBench inputs: 7-day action history, up to 5 news items from the prior 48h, selected fundamentals; ablation: removing news then fundamentals lowered returns for the models tested (Kimi-K2, GPT-OSS-120B). Failure modes were arithmetic errors in share counts and JSON schema violations — [StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)
- LiveTradeBench: reasoning-focused models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-Thinking, o3) had higher volatility without a trading advantage; models showed distinct risk appetites — [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)
- FINSABER regime behaviour: LLM timing strategies under-participate in bull markets and over-participate in bear markets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)

### Inferences
- The prompt gives the LLM standard retail-TA features plus OI and funding, which are already free for the team. Alpha Arena therefore tests "LLM as a discretionary TA trader with leverage", not any new information source. No evidence emerged that the LLM extracted information beyond what these features hold.
- Fee drag is mechanically predictable: at 0.02-0.05% per side plus slippage, 13 trades/day on a small account can consume 10%+ in two weeks. This is a cost-model sanity check, not an LLM finding.
- The most defensible regularity across sources is "turnover and leverage differ widely between LLMs given the same prompt, and the high-leverage/high-turnover ones did worst in this window". That is one regime, six data points.

### Gaps
- No audited per-trade data (entry/exit, fees, funding, holding time) was retrieved for any model; all behaviour descriptions are second-hand.
- The official nof1 system prompt is not public; only the third-party reconstruction exists.
- No primary breakdown of what share of P&L came from funding, fees or directional moves.

## 3. Is there published analysis of why some models won (Alpha Arena, Qwen/DeepSeek commentary)?

### Takeaway
The explanations on offer are narrative: moderate trade count and patience (Qwen, DeepSeek), leverage amplifying whatever direction was right, and market regime. None come with controlled evidence. The one principled stance is the organisers' own: early results may be luck, and the test lacked news. The mid-season reversal of DeepSeek's lead argues against any stable "Chinese models are better traders" story. Season 1.5's winner was a pre-release Grok variant, with no mechanism published.

### Cited Findings
- Explanations given: Qwen3-Max placed fewer, longer-held trades and benefited from leverage on better decisions; weaker strategies were punished by leverage; the Season 1 review lists poor directional calls, oversized positions, weak exit discipline, over-trading, prompt interpretation, latency and unfavourable regime as candidate causes without attributing weights — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/), [search-summary of leaderboard write-ups](https://blog.mexc.com/news/race-against-time-how-ai-models-battled-live-crypto-markets-in-alpha-arena)
- Same iWeaver review: "The article does not show that a model's origin or architecture explains the results" (summary of the review's own caveat) — [iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)
- Mid-season: Qwen led DeepSeek for a time in the final stretch and finished first; DeepSeek led on 27 Oct at +120% — [ForkLog 27 Oct](https://forklog.com/en/deepseek-surpasses-gpt-5-in-cryptocurrency-trading-competition/), [SCMP summary](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3330461/deepseek-and-qwen-ai-models-crush-western-rivals-cryptocurrency-trading-challenge)
- LiveTradeBench: LMArena rank does not predict trading return (Spearman 0.054 stocks, -0.38 Polymarket, n=21, no p-values) — [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)
- StockBench: reasoning-tuned models did not consistently beat instruct versions; rankings flipped between a downturn window and an upturn window — [StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)
- Season 1.5 winner (Grok 4.20) outperformed in all four competitions on US equities; no published explanation of mechanism — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)

### Inferences
- A pooled reading (Alpha Arena rank reversals, StockBench window flips, LiveTradeBench near-zero rank correlation across markets) is that model identity has little persistent explanatory power over trading P&L at these horizons; sizing/leverage and regime dominate.
- No source supports "Qwen/DeepSeek are better at trading" as a causal claim. If the team wanted to test any such hypothesis, the missing design elements are many seeds per model, a random/buy-and-hold null at matched leverage, and multiple windows.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or quantitative post-mortem of Alpha Arena Season 1 or 1.5 was found (no attribution of P&L to direction vs leverage vs fees).
- No evidence on whether Season 1.5's Grok result persisted; no Season 2 data.

## 4. What is reusable as a hypothesis or infrastructure (LLM news classification, multi-agent debate) versus noise?

### Takeaway
The credible, reusable pieces are (a) LLM as an auditable text-to-feature classifier (news headline to event type / sentiment score) feeding a conventional, pre-registered, cost-aware signal, supported by Lopez-Lira and Tang with decay as adoption grows; (b) infrastructure hygiene from the critiques: point-in-time data, post-cutoff evaluation, anonymisation, trial-count deflation, hash-chained decision logs. End-to-end "LLM decides and sizes the trade" and multi-agent debate show no independent evidence of edge. For this team, which must add independent information or shorten time-to-answer, the best fit is LLM-as-news-classifier on the existing RSS watch, plus using the critiques' protocols.

### Cited Findings
- LLM headline scoring: Lopez-Lira and Tang ("Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?", first version April 2023, latest 30 Oct 2025): ChatGPT-assigned good/bad/neutral scores for news headlines correlate positively with next-day returns, strongest for small caps and bad news (limits to arbitrage); weaker or absent for GPT-1/2/BERT; the October 2025 version tests post-cutoff headlines and says strategy returns fall as LLM adoption grows. Statements come from a search-tool summary of the paper, not a full read — [arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v5)
- Position paper "The Alpha Illusion" (arXiv 2605.16895) argues reported alpha from end-to-end LLM trading agents (FinCon, FinMem, TradingAgents, FinAgent, QuantAgent, FLAG-Trader) cannot yet be separated from temporal contamination, unmodelled frictions, short-window noise and parametric priors; proposes six validity tests and a P1-P6 reporting protocol, and recommends using LLMs as auditable information interfaces upstream of independent calibration, risk and execution modules. Abstract-level only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895)
- "Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?" (arXiv 2603.17692): anonymising tickers/company names, a policy built on LLM-agent scores plus GNN and PPO reaches Sharpe 1.40 ± 0.22 across 20 seeds on 2025 YTD to 1 Aug 2025 with negative-control tests, but alpha shrinks in trending bull markets; abstract-level only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17692)
- Gençay (arXiv 2608.27734): tool-registry that excludes look-ahead by construction plus deflation by logged trial count; even a leaky "Sharpe 35" oracle can pass deflation tests, so both guardrail and statistics are needed; all LLM-discovered strategies rejected — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)
- Look-Ahead-Bench (Benhenda, 2026) compares an in-training-data period (Apr-Sep 2021) with a post-cutoff period (Jul-Dec 2024), both with ~25% buy-and-hold, so performance gaps indicate bias; known only via a secondary summary — [search result summary](https://nazymaltbridge.substack.com/p/your-ai-backtesting-can-cheat-here) (not fetched)
- Multi-agent debate: TradingAgents is the main example; its reported edge is a 3-month, 3-stock backtest inside LLM training data with an implausible Sharpe; the Alpha Illusion paper lists "multi-agent disaggregation" as a required validity test — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138), [Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16895). I found no ablation showing the debate component itself adds return.
- Infrastructure ideas seen in repos: hash-chained append-only decision ledger, approval step and kill switch (ai-hedge-fund paper-trading mode) — [GitHub](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund); public per-wallet trade transparency (Alpha Arena); structured JSON decision schema with explicit invalidation conditions (reverse-engineered prompt) — [gist](https://gist.github.com/wquguru/7d268099b8c04b7e5b6ad6fae922ae83)

### Inferences
Testable ideas, framed for this repo's pre-registration rules (all are hypotheses to be declared before any data is viewed, not recommendations to trade):
1. LLM news-to-event-type classifier on the existing RSS watch: map headlines to a fixed, pre-registered taxonomy (exchange hack, listing, regulatory action, ETF flow, macro surprise), run once per headline at ingest time with the model version and prompt hashed and timestamped, then test next-hour/next-day abnormal return of the classes in forward-only data. Independent information (text) and slow-to-answer, so pair it with daily-P&L scoring. Constraint from the literature: decay with LLM adoption, strongest in small/illiquid names and after bad news; for crypto the analogue would be non-top-10 coins, chosen without looking at P&L.
2. Null-model benchmark for LLM traders: generate random-action traders at matched leverage/turnover/fees on the Alpha Arena window (data is public on Hyperliquid) to quantify how often 6 random traders produce a spread like the one observed. This is an "inspect_data" exercise and could be done with existing Hyperliquid fetcher code; cheap and directly answers the "luck vs skill" question. Weak on breadth because it is the same crypto factor.
3. Alpha Arena wallet forensics: from public on-chain Hyperliquid fills, compute per-model turnover, fees, funding, hold time, long/short balance and leverage, and regress P&L on a passive BTC/ETH/SOL long-short exposure. Expected finding (hypothesis, not evidence): P&L is largely explained by net direction times leverage. Does not add independent breadth for the strategy work, but would show whether anything beyond beta exists.
4. Prompt-feature check: OI and funding are in the Alpha Arena prompt; the team already collects funding. A low-cost sanity check is whether LLM decisions in the public logs correlate with funding sign (carry-aware or carry-blind). Hypothesis only.
5. Protocol imports: (a) blindfolding (anonymised tickers) and post-cutoff-only evaluation for any LLM-derived feature; (b) logging every LLM call and deflating by number of prompt variants tried (Gençay); (c) a hash-chained decision ledger for the news-RSS watch.
6. Noise to avoid: leaderboard-style "which model trades best" tracking, multi-agent debate trading systems, and copying prompt designs. No source shows these add information beyond TA features the team has already tested, and the evidence-rate rule in CLAUDE.md (no new trend/regime variants on the same coins) rules out re-running discretionary-TA variants on the same crypto set.

### Gaps
