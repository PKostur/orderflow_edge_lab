Research date: 2026-10-09. Tooling limits: WebSearch returned mostly vendor/aggregator pages; direct YouTube pages could not be read (a YouTube search-results fetch returned only footer text), and Bloomberg/Medium returned 403. So creator-level evidence is thin; this is stated plainly in each Gaps section. Nothing here is an edge claim. All fetched content treated as data.

## Q1. Which notable creators/videos/posts claim bots (title, date, URL), and for each: strategy, venue, cost assumptions, results, public code or verifiable track record?

### Takeaway

## Q2. Which recurring strategy families appear, and what do independent sources say about real profitability after fees?

### Takeaway
Prediction-market bots dominate recent claims. Independent on-chain evidence says profits concentrate in market makers and a few latency/arbitrage wallets, while takers (most retail bots) lose, and Polymarket added taker fees on 15-minute crypto markets in Jan 2026 to blunt latency arbitrage. Funding carry has compressed sharply (BTC perp funding avg ~2.2% in 2026 YTD vs 4.9% in 2025, 11% in 2024, per a secondary source), and no independent after-fee track record for retail funding-arb bots was found. Grid, copy-trading and liquidation-hunting have essentially only vendor or anecdotal support.

### Cited Findings

**Prediction-market arbitrage / bots**
- IMDEA Networks paper (Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer, Suarez-Tangil; arXiv 2508.03474, submitted 2025-08-05): two arbitrage forms, market rebalancing (within a market) and combinatorial (across related markets); estimated ~USD 40M realized arbitrage profit on Polymarket from on-chain order-book data. Data window (secondary outlets say Apr 2024-Apr 2025; one says 2023-24) is not on the abstract page — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)
- Secondary summary of that paper: 86M bets analyzed; top 3 wallets earned ~$4.2M from >10,200 bets; typical arbitrage yield 1-5%; most frequent in sports, largest profits in 2024 election politics — [search summary of IMDEA study](https://stafforini.com/works/craig-2025-polymarket-users-lost/) (secondary; verify against the paper)
- Telonex on-chain study, 2026-02-02 to 02-08: 46,945 wallets in 2,688 resolved 15-minute BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP markets, 15.3M fills, $153M volume. Only 36.8% of wallets profitable, median wallet ~-$3. Takers won 53% of fills yet netted -$728,501; makers netted +$728,501. 19 of top 20 wallets traded mainly BTC; top wallet +$270,569. Two archetypes: snipers (few markets, large PnL, possibly external price signals) and 100%-maker bots across 2,000+ markets. All PnL is PRE-fee (author: real taker profitability worse, maker slightly better); single week only — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
- Polymarket 15-minute crypto markets got taker-only fees in Jan 2026 (The Block, 2026-01-06); fees peak at 50% probability and fall toward 0/100; fees fund a Maker Rebates Program paid daily; The Block says docs suggest up to ~3% — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates)
- Current Polymarket docs: maker fee-equivalent = C x feeRate x p x (1-p); taker fee rate 0.07 for Crypto (maker rebate share 20%), 0.05 Sports (15%), 0.04 Finance/Politics (25%), Geopolitics fee-free; no effective dates given and rebate % is at Polymarket's discretion. Example: 100 shares at 0.50 crypto = 1.75 fee-equivalent (~3.5% of $50 notional) — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/market-makers/maker-rebates-program)
- Medium/Illumination author (unverified, no method) claims arbitrage windows shrank from 12.3s (2024) to 2.7s, 73% of arbitrage profit captured by sub-100ms bots, median spread 0.3% — treat as low confidence — [Medium](https://medium.com/illumination/beyond-simple-arbitrage-4-polymarket-strategies-bots-actually-profit-from-in-2026-ddacc92c5b4f) (page itself returned 403; figures come via search summary)
- Cross-venue Kalshi/Polymarket arbitrage: spread claims 1.5-5% with fees ignored in the source post; contract-rule mismatches and one-sided fills are the named risks — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/realfishsam/how-i-built-a-risk-free-arbitrage-bot-for-polymarket-kalshi-4f)
- Directional momentum bot on Polymarket lost money live (PF 0.58 over 104 trades) — [falsifylab](https://falsifylab.substack.com/p/strategy-deep-dive-polymarket)
- Polymarket market making: maker-only, earns spread plus rewards/rebates; the leading open-source implementation's author warns it can lose money — [GitHub poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)

**Funding arbitrage / basis / delta-neutral**
- Mechanics and fee drag: gross carry e.g. 0.012%/8h = 13.14% annualized; two-leg round trip costs ~0.16-0.2% of notional at taker rates (the guide's own illustration: ~5-6% net on capital before slippage), highly sensitive to rotation frequency and fee tier; Tread docs list ~2 bp fixed fee plus exchange maker fees per leg on Hyperliquid etc. — [Sharpe.ai guide](https://www.sharpe.ai/learn/funding-rate-arbitrage), [Tread docs](https://docs.tread.fi/delta-neutral-bot) (the net-return illustration is the search tool's own arithmetic, not a measured result)
- Ethena sUSDe (the largest real-money delta-neutral funding carry): per a secondary support page, 7-day trailing APY 7.1% in June 2026, 9.4% in April; 90-day avg 11.8% in April; range 4.1% (Aug 2024) to 35.2% (Q1 2024). Another summary: BTC perp funding avg 2.2% YTD through Aug 11 (2026) vs 4.9% in 2025 and 11% in 2024. Only ~11% of USDe backing now sits in the perp trade; Ethena shifts to stablecoins/treasuries when funding is below T-bill yields. In Oct 2025 crash funding flipped negative and USDe depegged (accounts differ: $0.65 on Binance vs $0.97) — [eco.com support page](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15254002-ethena-usde-and-susde-2026-delta-neutral-yield), [Stablecoin Insider Q1 2026](https://stablecoininsider.org/ethena-usde-q1-2026-report/) (secondary; conflicting numbers)
- Equity perps funding reported at ~14% (Hyperliquid) and ~17.5% (Binance) average recently, per the same Ethena coverage — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/ethena-is-targeting-the-120-trillion-wall-street-stocks-market-to-hunt-yields-5x-higher-than-bitcoin/) (secondary; not independently checked)
- Self-reported Hyperliquid vault DeltaUSD 17.0% lifetime with 0% fee lines — [tradingstrategy.ai](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/deltausd-hyperliquid-usdn-funding-arb)

**Grid bots**
- No rigorous crypto study found. A single Binance Square SUI/USDT grid backtest showed +197.52 USDT grid profit yet -33.31% total ROI and 45.59% max drawdown; broker/vendor pieces say grids lose in strong trends and claim 15-30%/yr in sideways markets (unsourced); forex-only academic backtests exist — [Investortrip](https://www.investortrip.com/articles/forex-brokers/grid-trading-strategy), [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/bg/square/hashtag/gridbot)

**Liquidation cascades**
- No open-source Hyperliquid liquidation-hunting bot with backtest found. An Oct 10, 2025 preprint estimates ~$641M force-sold on Hyperliquid at 21:19 UTC, ~$576M to the backstop and ~$64M to the order book; a separate analysis cites ETH spread widening from ~0.5 bp to ~301 bp and bid depth falling ~99.8%, implying mid-price fills are unrealistic in cascades — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/shifting-576-million-of-forced-sales-off-public-order-books-saved-hyperliquid-from-a-systemic-crash-during-peak-panic/), [Research Square preprint (title only confirmed)](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9459584/v1)
- Hyperliquid liquidations use a mark price from an aggregated index and may be taken by a liquidator vault; commercial liquidation-map APIs exist (Buildix) — [OneKey](https://onekey.so/blog/ecosystem/liquidation-cascade-hyperliquid/), [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/liquidation-map-api-hyperliquid-bots-traders-buildix-2026)

**Copy trading**
- No independent test that following top Hyperliquid wallets pays after latency/fees/selection bias; all sources are tool vendors — [Nansen docs](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-2-find-and-copytrade-wallets-on-hyperliquid)

**Generic skepticism**
- A Medium piece cites a viral 5,000% backtest that barely broke even under walk-forward testing; the same type of "backtest/live" gap is attributed to spread, slippage, latency, missing fees — [Medium](https://medium.com/@trading.dude/is-algorithmic-trading-for-retail-traders-a-scam-or-the-real-deal-d4277a4f26aa), [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/17747/differences-between-backtest-and-live/)
- MEV/sniping bots: not researched in depth; noted only as out of scope for this team (no search done).

### Inferences
- Where independent data exists (Telonex), the money flows to makers and a few low-latency wallets; a strategy that takes liquidity on short Polymarket crypto markets starts at a structural disadvantage, now larger after the 2026 taker fees.
- The "free arbitrage" posts (Poly-Kalshi, YES+NO < $1) either ignore fees or show windows of seconds; the independent $40M figure is gross and concentrated in a few wallets, so a retail-scale replication is not evidenced.
- Funding carry yields have shrunk to low single digits for BTC (secondary data), consistent with the team's own finding that carry did not replicate on consistent-venue funding. Creator funding-arb claims quoting 10-20% likely reflect past regimes or small-cap/equity perps, with higher liquidation, venue and basis risk.
- Grid and copy-trading have no independent after-fee evidence; they are low priority.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or audited after-fee returns for retail funding-arb bots, grid bots, Hyperliquid copy-trading, or liquidation-hunting.
- Exact IMDEA data window and fee treatment unverified (abstract page only).
- Ethena numbers come from secondary support/analysis pages with internal conflicts.
- Polymarket fee schedule history (when the 0.07 crypto rate started, whether it differs from Jan 2026) not established; The Block and docs figures differ in framing.

## Q3. Which of these rely on free, historically available data versus forward-only or paid data?

### Takeaway
Free historical data exists for Polymarket (on-chain Polygon logs, a ~1 TB public Hugging Face dataset, Gamma API), Hyperliquid funding/candles (public API), and exchange funding histories. Order-book depth, L2 queue data, liquidation streams and wallet-labelling are forward-only or paid/vendor-gated. Much of the independent prediction-market evidence uses a paid dataset (Telonex).

### Cited Findings
- Polymarket history: Entriken's dataset (~1 TB, ~30 tables incl. markets, accounts, fills, built from blockchain logs rather than the Gamma API, Parquet/DuckDB) is public on Hugging Face and the code is on GitHub; blog also notes an API provider at ~$30/month or a free key — [blog.phor.net](https://blog.phor.net/polymarket-quant-desk)
- Telonex's wallet study used Telonex's own on-chain fills dataset (15.3M fills) — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m) (whether the dataset is free was not established)
- poly-maker has no backtester yet and relies on captured journals, i.e. forward-collected data — [GitHub poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)
- The IMDEA study used on-chain historical order-book data (their reconstruction) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)
- Hyperliquid ledger (trades, balances, liquidations) is public on-chain, so any claimed bot wallet is auditable if the address is published — [FundingArb vendor claim via search](https://fundingarb.net/) (vendor statement; not checked against Hyperliquid docs)
- Liquidation analytics (liquidation maps/cascade simulators) are commercial APIs (Buildix) or require building a monitor (GoldRush guide) — [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/liquidation-map-api-hyperliquid-bots-traders-buildix-2026), [GoldRush](https://goldrush.dev/guides/building-a-real-time-hyperliquid-liquidation-monitor-part-1/)
- Cascade backtests need depth/spread data (ETH spread 0.5 bp to ~301 bp, bid depth -99.8% on Oct 10, 2025); mid-price fills would be badly optimistic — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/shifting-576-million-of-forced-sales-off-public-order-books-saved-hyperliquid-from-a-systemic-crash-during-peak-panic/)
- Copy-trading wallet leaderboards come from vendors (Nansen, Apify, Hyperbot) — [Nansen docs](https://docs.nansen.ai/guides/templates/complex-use-cases/use-case-2-find-and-copytrade-wallets-on-hyperliquid)

### Inferences (for the team's constraints: free data, pre-registration, no order placement)
- Ranked by testability with free historical data: (1) Polymarket on-chain wallet/fill studies, e.g. maker vs taker PnL by market type, using the public Hugging Face dataset or Polygon logs (the Telonex week is a pre-fee, single-week example; a pre-registered replication on a longer window with the fee schedule applied is the obvious follow-up); (2) IMDEA-style YES+NO < $1 rebalancing frequency and duration on that dataset, with fees; (3) Hyperliquid vs other-venue funding replication (already in progress). These add independent information (prediction markets are outside the crypto factor) and can be scored on daily or per-market P&L.
- Forward-only: liquidation-cascade microstructure, queue-position-dependent market making, and copy-trading latency studies; these need the OKX/other collectors already underway or paid data.
- Caveat: these are descriptive-research ideas only, consistent with no live trading or order placement; I make no edge claim and none is supported by the sources.

### Gaps
- Whether the Telonex dataset or Hugging Face dataset covers the 15-minute markets over a long window, their license, and coverage start date were not verified.
- Hyperliquid API data retention limits (funding, L2) not checked against official docs.
- Kalshi historical data availability (free vs paid) not researched.
- Cost/availability of Polymarket Gamma/CLOB historical order-book snapshots not established.
